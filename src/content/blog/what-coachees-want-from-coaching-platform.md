Most coaching platform content is written for coaches and the people who buy coaching programmes. Makes sense. They're the ones evaluating tools and making purchasing decisions.

But there's a whole other user who rarely gets asked what they want: the coachee.

We've spent a lot of time talking to people on the receiving end of coaching engagements. What they care about is surprisingly consistent, and it's often different from what coaches and admins assume.

## They want to see their own progress

This comes up constantly. Coachees want to look at something and feel like they're moving forward. That might be assessment scores improving over time, goals being ticked off, or simply a clear record of what's been covered across sessions.

Without that visibility, coaching can feel abstract. "I think I'm getting better at giving feedback, but am I really?" When there's a concrete record, even a simple one, it anchors the experience. Progress becomes something you can point to, not just something you vaguely sense.

Most coaching tools give this visibility to the coach and the admin. The coachee gets a calendar invite and maybe a feedback form. That's backwards.

## They want things to be easy

This sounds obvious, but the bar is low and a lot of platforms still miss it.

Coachees aren't logging into your platform every day. They might open it once a week, or once before each session. Every time they do, they need to quickly orient themselves. Where are my upcoming sessions? What did we talk about last time? Is there anything I need to do before the next one?

If the answer to any of those questions requires clicking through multiple screens or decoding an unintuitive interface, they'll stop engaging. Not out of laziness, but because they have a hundred other things competing for their attention. The platform needs to respect that.

Pre-session prompts, clear timelines, and a simple dashboard go a long way. Nothing revolutionary. Just thoughtful design.

## They want a private space for reflection

Here's where it gets interesting. Many coachees told us they want a place to write things down between sessions. Thoughts, observations, things they tried and how it went. Not because their coach asked them to, but because the coaching process surfaces things they want to capture before they forget.

But they'll only do this if they trust that the space is genuinely private. The moment there's ambiguity about who can read their reflections, they stop writing honest ones. Some platforms make this worse by lumping coaching notes into systems that HR or managers can access. Even the perception of visibility changes behaviour.

A good coaching platform makes it crystal clear: this is your space, your coach can see what you choose to share, and nobody else gets access to your personal reflections.

## They don't want to feel monitored

This is the big one. Coaching works because it's a relationship built on trust. The coachee opens up about struggles, blind spots, and areas where they feel uncertain. That openness is what makes the whole thing valuable.

If the platform feels like a tracking system, that trust erodes. Coachees start wondering: is my manager going to see this? Is the organisation using this data to evaluate me? Am I being scored?

The design of the platform communicates a lot. Dashboards covered in metrics and admin controls signal oversight. A clean, personal interface that centres the coachee's own journey signals support.

Sometimes it's the small things. Calling something "your development journal" instead of "coaching log." Showing coachees their own assessment trends before anyone else sees them. Letting them control what gets shared in summary reports.

## They want the platform to feel like it's for them

This is the thread that runs through everything. Coachees want to feel like the platform exists to support their growth, not to generate reports for someone else.

That doesn't mean reporting and admin features aren't important. They are. Organisations sponsoring coaching programmes need to understand outcomes. But those features should be invisible to the coachee. When a coachee logs in, they should see their goals, their sessions, their progress, and their reflections.

Their experience. Their journey.

When platforms get this right, engagement goes up. Coachees prepare for sessions, complete assessments on time, and actually use the tools between meetings. They become active participants rather than passive recipients.

And that's when coaching really works.
