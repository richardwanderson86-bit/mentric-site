Every coaching practice starts with spreadsheets. That's fine. When you have three clients and a simple workflow, a well-organised Google Sheet is genuinely all you need. No shame in it.

But there's a moment where spreadsheets stop being helpful and start being a liability. Most coaches blow right past that moment without noticing, because the pain builds gradually. One more tab. One more colour code. One more "I'll fix the formatting later."

Here are five signs you've crossed that line.

## 1. You have multiple versions of the same tracker

There's the one on your laptop. There's the one in Google Drive that you shared with a colleague six months ago. There's the one you duplicated to try a new layout and never deleted. They've all diverged. You're not entirely sure which one is current, and honestly, checking feels like too much effort.

If this sounds familiar, your system has outgrown single-file storage. You need one source of truth, not three competing ones.

## 2. A coachee asks for their data and you feel a small wave of panic

"Could you send me a summary of what we've covered?" Perfectly reasonable request. Should take two minutes.

Instead, you're scrolling through tabs, squinting at date columns, trying to remember if the notes from that March session were in the spreadsheet or in an email you sent yourself. You piece something together, but it takes twenty minutes and you're not confident you got everything.

Your coachees deserve better. And frankly, so does your time.

## 3. You can't answer basic questions about your practice

How many active clients do you have right now? What's your average engagement length? Which assessments have you used most this quarter?

If answering any of those requires you to manually count rows or build a pivot table, you're doing work that software should do for you. These aren't exotic analytics. They're the basics, and they should be available at a glance.

## 4. Session notes live in three different places

Some are in the spreadsheet. Some are in a notebook. Some are in a Google Doc you created for that one client who needed extra detail. A few are in your email drafts because you started writing them up and got interrupted.

Scattered notes aren't just inconvenient. They break continuity. When you sit down before a session and can't quickly review what happened last time, the coaching suffers. You end up asking questions you've already asked. You miss threads that were building momentum.

Good coaching requires good memory, and "good memory" in practice means having your notes in one searchable place.

## 5. You spend more time managing the spreadsheet than using it

Adding a new client means duplicating a template sheet, renaming it, updating the index tab, and adjusting the formulas. Colour-coding is now a twenty-minute ritual. You've considered building a macro but you're not sure how.

When the tool requires its own maintenance routine, it's no longer saving you time. It's a side project. And you didn't become a coach to maintain spreadsheets.

## What comes next

None of this means you were wrong to start with spreadsheets. They're flexible, familiar, and free. But a growing practice has different needs: structured session tracking, a single place for notes and assessments, basic reporting that updates itself, and a way for coachees to access their own information without you playing middleman.

That's what a coaching platform is for. Not to add complexity, but to remove the kind of complexity that spreadsheets create as they scale.

If you recognised yourself in two or more of these signs, it might be time to make the switch. Your future self, mid-session and looking for last month's notes, will thank you.
