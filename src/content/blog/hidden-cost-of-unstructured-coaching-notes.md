You finished a great session. The coachee had a real breakthrough about how they handle conflict on their team. You jot a few lines in a Google Doc, maybe send yourself an email with a reminder to follow up on it next time.

Three weeks later, you're prepping for the next session. Where did you write that down?

This is the hidden cost of unstructured notes. Not a dramatic failure. Just a slow, quiet erosion of coaching quality that's hard to notice until it's already happened.

## Continuity is the first casualty

Coaching builds on itself. Session four references something from session two. A pattern that started emerging in month one becomes the focus of month three. Lose the thread, and you're essentially starting fresh each time.

When notes live in random documents, notebooks, and apps, maintaining that thread takes real effort. You spend the first few minutes of each session reconstructing context instead of building on it. Your coachee notices, even if they don't say anything.

## You can't spot patterns you can't see

One of the most valuable things a coach does is notice patterns that the coachee can't see themselves. The same avoidance behaviour showing up in three different stories. A confidence shift that correlates with a change in how they talk about their team.

Spotting these patterns requires being able to look across sessions easily. If your notes are in five different places with five different formats, that kind of synthesis becomes nearly impossible. You're relying entirely on memory, and memory is unreliable.

## "What have we covered?" becomes a hard question

At some point, a coachee or a programme sponsor will ask for a summary. What themes have you worked on? What progress has been made? What's the plan going forward?

With structured notes in one place, this takes minutes. Without them, it takes an afternoon of archaeology. Digging through folders, scanning email threads, trying to reconstruct a timeline from fragments.

And the result is never as good. You miss things. You forget that pivotal conversation in session five because the notes were in a physical notebook that's now in a different bag.

## The fix isn't complicated

This isn't a problem that requires a massive process overhaul. It requires one decision: all coaching notes go in one place, with a consistent structure.

That structure doesn't need to be elaborate. Date, client, key themes discussed, actions agreed, anything to follow up on. That's enough. The important thing is consistency and searchability.

A dedicated coaching platform handles this by design. Notes are tied to sessions, sessions are tied to clients, and everything is searchable and timestamped. But even if you're not ready for a platform, just consolidating into a single structured document per client is a significant upgrade over the status quo.

The cost of unstructured notes isn't obvious. Nobody's going to tell you that your coaching suffered because you couldn't find a note from two months ago. But over time, the quality gap between a coach who can instantly review the full history and one who's working from fragments is real.

Your notes are the backbone of continuity. Treat them like it.
